# OpenBGA Lab Ops safeguards reference

This page lists the safeguards that the [roadmap](roadmap.md) cites. For each one it states what
the application records or enforces in this release and what stays with the laboratory and its
host. It describes the software. It does not say that a laboratory complies with any rule, and no
part of the application scores or certifies compliance. Each laboratory decides which rules apply
to it and makes its own assessment.

The citations are United States federal text: 45 CFR Part 164, the HIPAA Security and Privacy
Rules, in the [eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/part-164) as of 2026-11-16, read on
2026-11-18; §164.312 was checked again on 2026-11-24 (last amended 2016-12-30), and §164.308(a)(1)(ii)(D) and §164.316(b) on 2026-11-25. "Required" and "Addressable" are the rule's own labels. For an addressable
specification the laboratory assesses whether it is reasonable and appropriate. It then implements
it, or documents why not and implements an equivalent alternative where that is reasonable and
appropriate (§164.306(d)(3)). The proposed changes to the Security Rule (90 FR 898, January 2025)
are not in force and are not described here.

The **Security settings** page in the application (Setup, administrators) shows the values in
force in a running deployment: password rules, failed sign-in limits, the session limits, which
accounts must have a second sign-in factor, who can view patient information or use emergency
access, whether staff
confirm it is them before revealing patient information, and the connection settings, with who
sets each one. Administrators set the session limits, the second-factor rule and the confirmation
before revealing patient information there. The statements below on
passwords, failed sign-ins, sessions and re-authentication describe this release's default
configuration (`config/settings.py`). The Security settings page follows the deployment settings
read from the environment; where a deployment has changed a setting in `config/settings.py` that
a statement depends on, the page says so and names the setting instead of describing it. The laboratory's
security contact and privacy contact are recorded on the laboratory profile, where every staff
member can read them.

Each roadmap item that changes a safeguard updates its row here in the same change and adds the
laboratory's settings for it to the Security settings page.

## Administrative safeguards, §164.308

| Safeguard | Lab Ops today | Stays with the laboratory and its host | Planned |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Information system activity review (Required), (a)(1)(ii)(D) | Changes are recorded as attributed, append-only change events. Revealing a patient-marked answer or a person-level key, and an exact-key search, are recorded too. Each change event recorded from this release on keeps the ID of the request that made it, which the server's access log carries. Each time a staff or client portal account opens a ticket, its audit history, its Who opened it page, its Form details page or one of its complaint forms, opens a new ticket with Request again from it, downloads a file, is shown a patient-marked answer (a staff reveal, or a portal account's own ticket page), reveals a ticket's person-level key or is shown a ticket's entries on a client organization's timeline, an access record keeps the account, the time, the capacity (laboratory staff or client portal), the ticket, the file or answer, and the request ID, never what was shown; the database refuses to change or delete one, a refused read records nothing and a page whose record cannot be written is not shown. Rows shown in a list or a search, the search text, the action pages that show only a ticket's number and a HEAD request are not recorded. Supervisors read the change history of one ticket, which does not list reveals, and its access records on Who opened it. Completed sign-ins, refused passwords, wrong codes entered for an existing account, sign-outs made with Sign out and sessions ended by the session limits, a deactivation, a new password, the person from another session or a superuser are passed to the application's sign-in log, with the username when the account is known; some refusals are not (a code entered after its emailed sign-in attempt expired, a code for an address with no account, a request stopped by the request limit). Each account's sign-ins, sign-outs, failed and refused sign-in attempts and failed re-authentications are also kept in the database as its sign-in history, which the database refuses to change or delete; a failed password is kept only when the username or email address given names exactly one account. Each person reads their own entries of the last 90 days on Sign-in history. Staff with a laboratory role who hold the explicit capability Can review access and audit records, which a superuser grants to a person or to a role group and no role has otherwise, read every change event, access record and account's sign-in history in the Audit review workspace, newest first, filtered by person, ticket, client organization, action and dates, and export them as a spreadsheet file (at most 10,000 rows), also limited to what happened outside the opening hours of a business calendar; its People tab counts, per account over a period of at most 366 days, sign-ins, failed or refused sign-ins, tickets opened, files downloaded, patient information shown and changes made, and how many fell outside business hours, each count opening the rows it counted. Its rows name who, what, which record and when, never a change's before and after values or reason or what was shown, and a shared demonstration account's browser and address are left out. The workspace changes nothing it shows. Each page of it shown and each export is recorded with the account, the time, the list, the filters accepted (never text as typed), the rows matched and the request ID, and listed there as Workspace use; the database refuses to change or delete these records, and a page or export whose record cannot be written is not sent. On the workspace's Reviews tab a holder of the capability records each review as a written record (§164.316(b)(1)(ii)): the period covered (at most 366 days, ending today or earlier), the lists or other records examined, the outcome, findings and follow-up, signed in the same step with the reviewer's password entered again (the second factor is not asked; a password not accepted signs nothing and is kept in the sign-in history as a failed re-authentication). The review keeps the signer's name, username and roles as they stood, the meaning Reviewed, the time and a SHA-256 checksum of what was signed, which its page takes again to show whether the stored review still matches; the database refuses to change or delete a review or a note added to it later. The tab shows the review interval in days that a reviewer sets with a reason (none ships), when the next review is due counted from the last signing, the last day reviewed and the days between reviewed periods that no review covers. The application sends no reminder when a review is due, asks for no second approval and deletes no review. | The review procedure: what to examine, how often (the interval entered in the application is the laboratory's choice) and what to do about findings; who is given the review capability; how long to keep review records (six years under §164.316(b)(2)(i)). Retention and review of the host's and proxy's logs. | |
| Authorization and supervision, workforce clearance, termination procedures (Addressable), (a)(3)(ii)(A)-(C) | Each person has a separate account. Work is limited by role, and patient information by an explicit grant that a superuser gives to a person or to a role group; no role has it otherwise. Deactivating an account stops its sign-in, refuses it at its next request and returns its open tickets to their team queues; a change the person is saving at that moment finishes first, and one they start afterwards is refused. Removing a role, a grant, a team membership or a portal site waits the same way; taking a capability from a role waits for its members one at a time, so a member it has not reached yet can still save a change before it. Deactivating also ends every session of the account at once, and they stay ended if it is made active again. A superuser can also end every session of an account from the admin console, with a reason, without deactivating it (on their own account, every one but the one in use). A superuser deactivates an account with Deactivate on its page, which shows what the account holds and asks for a reason. Every save that deactivates an account, there or by clearing Active, records in the same change what the account held just before (admin console access, roles, capabilities with how each is held, teams and portal access), who saved, when, the reason given on Deactivate and how many tickets went back to their team queues; the account's page shows the latest record, and the database refuses to change or delete one. A deactivated account keeps its roles and grants, which give nothing until it is made active again. Changes to accounts, roles and grants in the admin console are recorded with the values before and after; an account page opened before another change of that account refuses to save over it. Holders of the review capability list, under Access review, every account with its status, and a deactivated account with who deactivated it as the change history records it, and the active accounts with no sign-in for a period the laboratory sets (none ships); each page and export is recorded as Workspace use. On its Reviews tab they record an access review of the active staff accounts or the active portal accounts (of every organization or of one): every grant each account holds is listed and the reviewer decides Keep or Remove for each, adds findings and follow-up, and signs with their password entered again; the review keeps each decision, the signer's name, username and roles, the meaning Reviewed, the time and a SHA-256 checksum, and the database refuses to change or delete it. The review removes nothing: a superuser removes the grant, or deactivates the account, in the admin console, and the review's page shows whether each grant decided for removal is still held. A capability given directly to an account, and a portal account's access, may have a last day: from the start of the next day in the laboratory's time zone the grant gives nothing, by the database's clock at every check, and stays on the account until it is removed or a later day is set; the access report shows it as ended. Roles, admin console access and team memberships have no end date, nothing reminds anyone before an end, and nothing removes an ended grant. | Who may be given access, the clearance decision, which grants get a last day and which day, when and how often access is reviewed, and removing access when a person leaves or a review decides it, including ended grants still on an account. | |
| Access authorization; access establishment and modification (Addressable), (a)(4)(ii)(B)-(C) | Roles and the three explicit capabilities (patient information, access and audit review, and emergency access) are granted by a superuser in the admin console, and each grant or removal is a recorded change. Saving any change to an account or a role there asks the superuser for their password, and their second factor where they have one, again, unless they gave them in that browser in the last 5 minutes (Person or entity authentication, below). Restricted ticket categories limit a ticket to the responsible team's members and supervisors. Portal accounts are limited to their organization or listed sites. Holders of the review capability read the access report under Access review: every account with its roles, capabilities (directly or through a role or group), admin console access, teams, portal access and second factor, with who gave each role, capability and admin console access and when, as the change history records it, or "Not in the change history"; filtered and exported as a spreadsheet file. The report changes nothing. A signed access review on the Reviews tab records, for each grant the chosen accounts hold, the reviewer's decision to keep or remove it, with findings, follow-up and the sign-off; the change itself is made by a superuser in the admin console and is a recorded change, and the review's page shows whether each grant decided for removal is still held. A superuser may give a capability given directly a last day, and staff who maintain portal access may give a portal account's access one; setting or moving it is recorded in the account's or the access's change, and after it the grant gives nothing. An access review has no interval, reminder or second approver; roles and other grants have no end date. | The access policy, how often who holds what is reviewed, the last days of time-limited grants, and carrying out the removals a review decides. | Staff access by team (Later) |
| Protection from malicious software (Addressable), (a)(5)(ii)(B) | Uploaded files are checked for size, count, extension and the structure of their type. This is a format check, not a malware scan. | Malware protection on workstations, the host and any file scanning. | Attachment scanning (Later) |
| Log-in monitoring (Addressable), (a)(5)(ii)(C) | By default, failed sign-ins are limited per username or email address typed and per network address. The counts are kept in the database, so every server process of the deployment counts the same attempts, under a keyed digest of the name or address rather than the name or address itself; a count is removed once it expires, never to make room. A completed sign-in with a second factor, and a password reset, clear the failures counted for their network address, and the reset those counted for the account's email addresses. Completed sign-ins, refused passwords, wrong codes entered for an existing account, sign-outs made with Sign out and sessions ended by the session limits, a deactivation, a new password, the person from another session or a superuser are passed to the application's sign-in log with the username when the account is known; a code entered after its emailed sign-in attempt expired, a code for an address with no account and a request stopped by the request limit are not, and this release's logging configuration writes them to the console for the host to keep. The database keeps each account's last sign-in time and, for each session, when it started, the browser and address it reported at sign-in, when it was last active and when and why it ended. Each person sees their own open sessions on Where you are signed in and can sign out the others. Each account's sign-in history is kept in the database: every session's sign-in, with what it proved, and its sign-out, with why it ended, written by the database itself; a password, an authenticator or recovery code or an emailed code submitted and not accepted, at sign-in or when a signed-in page asks for it again (the re-authentication pages, Confirm it is you before a change to access or security settings, and the current password on Change password); an attempt refused by the failed sign-in limit before it was checked; and a sign-in refused because the account is deactivated or the password alone was given while marked as compromised, each with the browser and address the request reported. A failed or limited password attempt is kept only when the username or email address given names exactly one account; one naming none or several stays only in the sign-in log. A refused sign-in is kept for the account whose password or emailed code was accepted, and an account deactivated while a sign-in is under way refuses it. Refusals by the limit are kept at most once a minute for each account, step and address. No entry holds a password, a code or the typed username or email address. The database refuses to change or delete an entry, and entries have no retention period. Each person reads their own entries of the last 90 days, at most 100, on Sign-in history, with a warning counting the failures and refusals listed; a shared demonstration account sees other visitors' entries without their browser or address. Holders of the review capability read every account's history in the Audit review workspace, where the People tab counts each account's failed or refused attempts over a period (Information system activity review, above). Where the deployment sends email, the account holder is emailed when a superuser marks their password as compromised or removes their authenticator app or recovery codes, and when an authenticator app is added to the account, new recovery codes are issued or the app is removed from the account's own two-factor pages. The notice names the laboratory, the username, what changed and when (UTC), and "an administrator" where a superuser made the change. It tells the person to contact the laboratory's security contact as the profile records it (or the laboratory's administrator when none is recorded) if they did not make the change, and after a compromised mark says what to do next. It carries no code, secret, network address, browser or ticket content, and links only to the site's address. It is recorded with the change, retried by the scheduled jobs, sent whatever the person chose for their own email notices, recorded as not sent when email is off (as on the public demonstration), and not sent when the account is inactive or has no email address when it is sent. A password changed by its holder or set by a superuser, and a change of email address, send no notice. | Monitoring those logs and the history, and reporting discrepancies, including what a person reports after a notice they did not expect. | |
| Password management (Addressable), (a)(5)(ii)(D) | By default, a new password needs at least 15 characters, or 8 for an account that the second sign-in factor rule covers when the password is chosen. It must not be on Django's list of nearly 20,000 common passwords taken from breached ones, nor be a common password (where it has at least four letters) or a name of the laboratory, the application or the account with only digits, symbols, spaces or letter case changed, nor one character or a text of fewer than 15 characters repeated, nor a run such as 123456789, nor close to the account's username, name or email address. The whole password is compared each time, so a passphrase containing a common word is accepted. No rule asks for kinds of characters, and passwords never expire. Each password form states the rule for its account, and `setup_lab`, `changepassword` and the demonstration's seed check it too; Django's `createsuperuser` checks it only when it asks for the password. A reset link does not work while its account is a shared demonstration account. A password chosen before this rule keeps working until it is next changed, and so does one chosen while the second-factor rule covered the account after the rule stops covering it. A person resets a forgotten password by email when the deployment sends email. A new password ends the account's other sessions at once. A superuser marks an account's password as compromised from the admin console, with a reason (audited): its sessions end, the password no longer signs the account in on its own, a sign-in that also gives an authenticator code or an emailed code opens only Choose a new password (after setting up a second factor the laboratory requires), any new password lifts the mark, and the marked password is refused as a new one. Where the deployment sends email, the person is emailed that their password was marked and what to do next (Log-in monitoring, above). | The password policy and training, including what counts as evidence that a password is compromised and telling the person. Checking passwords against a breach service online, if wanted. | |
| Security incident procedures: response and reporting (Required), (a)(6)(ii) | Nothing specific. A nonconformance record, where that module is on, can describe a problem and its actions. | The incident procedure, its records and any breach notification. | Security incident and breach assessment records |
| Evaluation, (a)(8) | The Security settings page and this reference show what the application does, for the laboratory's evaluation. The [security policy](../SECURITY.md#what-is-checked) lists the checks the source and the container image go through, and each published release carries a software bill of materials of its image once that SBOM has passed its completeness check. | The periodic technical and nontechnical evaluation itself. | Each safeguards item updates both |
| Business associate contracts, (b) | Nothing. | Agreements with the host, the mail provider, backup storage and any other service that handles patient information. | Supplier and referral laboratory register (Later) |

## Technical safeguards, §164.312

| Safeguard | Lab Ops today | Stays with the laboratory and its host | Planned |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unique user identification (Required), (a)(2)(i) | Every account has its own username, and every change event names the account that made it. Shared demonstration accounts exist only on the public demonstration. | Giving each person their own account. | |
| Emergency access procedure (Required), (a)(2)(ii) | Staff who hold the explicit capability Can use emergency access, which a superuser gives to a person or to a role group and nobody holds until then, and who cannot view patient information, can start emergency access on a ticket they may read that holds patient information hidden from them: they choose a reason from a list, say in their own words what the emergency is, and confirm it is them (their password, and their second factor where they have one, unless given in that browser in the last 5 minutes). For 60 minutes from the start, by the database's clock and fixed in this release, they can reveal that ticket's patient-marked answers and person-level key one at a time; each reveal is recorded as any reveal is, and its change event names the use. It gives no other ticket, no exact-key search, no person-level link page and no change to patient information, and it stops at once when the capability, the role or the account is removed. Starting it records the use, with the person's name, username and roles, the ticket, the reason, the explanation, the start and the end, and a change event without the explanation; where the deployment sends email, every supervisor and every holder of the review capability with an email address, the person excepted, is emailed the ticket number and a sign-in link, whatever their own notice preference. Each use waits on the Emergency access tab of Access review until someone other than the person records a review, after it has ended, with an outcome and a note; the database refuses to change or delete a use or a review, a review by the person and a review of a use still in force. Opening a ticket the person cannot read, such as a restricted one, by emergency access is not built yet. Otherwise access follows roles and grants. | The emergency access procedure itself: who is given the capability, when emergency access may be used, who reviews the uses and how a use found not appropriate is followed up; and how necessary information is obtained when the application is unavailable. | Emergency access (opening a restricted ticket) |
| Automatic logoff (Addressable), (a)(2)(iii) | A session is signed out after a period without activity and after a longest session, both set by an administrator on the Security settings page, separately for staff and for client portal accounts, from 5 to 60 minutes and from 1 to 24 hours (30 minutes and 12 hours until set). The server checks both on every request. Opening a page, saving, searching or choosing Stay signed in is activity; reading or typing on a page is not. Pages warn 2 minutes before the sign-out for no activity and 5 minutes before the longest session ends; the admin console does not warn. Signing in again returns to the page. When a limit ends the session, what the person was typing in a ticket's reply, note or logged contact, a new ticket logged by staff, a portal reply, a new portal ticket or a nonconformance form is kept in the database for 24 hours, for that account in that browser only (a random value in a cookie of its own, holding nothing typed), never in the browser's storage, and given back once in the same form; files, fields that hold patient information and other forms are not kept, and Sign out discards it. Every page shown to a signed-in person is marked not to be stored by the browser. Without Remember me at a password sign-in, the session cookie also ends when the browser closes. Each person can see where they are signed in and sign out their other sessions, and a superuser can end a person's sessions with a reason. | The assessment of this specification, the periods chosen, and workstation locking. | |
| Encryption and decryption (Addressable), (a)(2)(iv) | Nothing inside the application. Authenticator secrets and recovery codes are stored unencrypted in the database. | Encryption of the database, its storage and its backups at the host, and protection of database dumps. | Encryption of sign-in secrets and patient information |
| Audit controls, (b) | Change events record who did what, when, to which record, why, and (from this release on) in which request. Revealing a person-level key or a patient-marked answer, and an exact-key search, are recorded without the value; a reveal under emergency access also names the use, and each use and its review are kept, append-only. Opening a ticket, its audit history, its form details or its complaint forms, downloading a file, being shown a patient-marked answer, by staff or by a portal account, revealing a person-level key, and being shown a ticket's entries on a client organization's timeline are kept as access records, apart from the change events, with the account, time, capacity, ticket, file or answer and request ID and without what was shown; the database refuses to change or delete them. Lists and searches are not recorded. Sign-ins, sign-outs and failed or refused sign-in attempts are kept per account as its sign-in history, append-only, which each person reads for their own account. The holders of the review capability examine all three across records in the Audit review workspace, as lists or as counts per person, either limited to what happened outside business hours if wanted; the workspace's own use is recorded the same way. | Log collection and retention at the host. Which reads to examine, and how long to keep access records: the application keeps them with no retention period. | Tamper-evident audit records |
| Integrity; mechanism to authenticate (Addressable), (c)(1)-(2) | Database triggers refuse changes and deletions of history, form values and file bytes. Files are stored with a SHA-256 checksum. Someone who controls the database can disable the triggers, so this is not tamper evidence. | Restricting database administration and keeping protected backups. | Tamper-evident audit records |
| Person or entity authentication, (d) | A password, and an authenticator app with recovery codes, which each account's holder can add; shared demonstration accounts cannot. An administrator can require one, on the Security settings page, of every staff account, staff with chosen roles, staff who can view patient information, accounts that can open the admin console and client portal accounts. An account the rule covers when it signs in, by password or emailed code, without an authenticator app sets one up before it can open anything else, and again whenever its account has none while the rule still covers it; sessions the rule did not cover at sign-in continue until they end. By default, adding or changing the second factor needs a recent re-authentication. Before a change to an account or a role in the admin console (adding either, an account's or a role's page, an account's password page, marking a password as compromised, removing an authenticator) or to the session limits, the second-factor rule or the confirmation before revealing patient information on Security settings, the application asks the person making it for their password where they have one, and a code from their authenticator app or a recovery code where they have one, again, unless they gave them in that browser in the last 5 minutes (`ACCOUNT_REAUTHENTICATION_TIMEOUT`) and still have that password and that authenticator; signing in does not count, and a new sign-in, even of the same account in the same browser, drops confirmations given in the earlier one. Without that confirmation nothing is saved and no change event is recorded. A password or code not accepted is kept in the person's sign-in history and counts toward the failed sign-in limit; what is typed is never stored, logged or put in a change event. An account with neither a password nor an authenticator cannot make these changes. Ending an account's sessions does not ask. Changes to a person's authenticator app or recovery codes, and a compromised mark, are emailed to the account holder where the deployment sends email (Log-in monitoring, above); adding an app, issuing new recovery codes or removing the app on the person's own two-factor pages is also recorded as a change event with each factor's id, type and times, never a secret or code. Where an administrator turns it on, on Security settings with a reason, the same confirmation (the password and, where the person has an authenticator app, a code from it or a recovery code; an account without a password gives the code alone) is asked before the first reveal of a patient-marked answer or a person-level key in each sign-in: until it is given, the reveal sends the person to Confirm it is you, and nothing is read, revealed or recorded. Once given, later reveals are not asked for the rest of that sign-in (until Sign out, a new sign-in, even of the same account in the same browser, a session limit, a new password set for the account or a deactivation). A confirmation given in that session in the last 5 minutes for another change counts, and the sign-in does not. Failed confirmations are kept and counted as above, and an account with neither a password nor an authenticator cannot reveal while it is on. It is off until saved, and not asked before a search by a person-level key or for what a client portal account is shown. No rule cited here requires it; whether to ask is the laboratory's choice. Where the deployment sends email, a code sent to the account's address can take the place of the password, and the second factor is still asked for. A new password needs 15 characters where it is the account's only factor and 8 where the rule covers the account (Password management, above). A password a superuser marks as compromised no longer signs the account in on its own. | Which accounts must have a second factor, whether to ask for a confirmation before revealing patient information, and the account recovery procedure when one is lost. | |
| Transmission security; integrity controls and encryption (Addressable), (e) | With debug mode off, plain HTTP is redirected to HTTPS, browsers are told to keep using HTTPS (for one hour unless the deployment sets `SECURE_HSTS_SECONDS`), and cookies are sent over HTTPS only. Email notices carry the ticket number, a fixed event description, the laboratory's name and a sign-in link, never ticket content. | TLS at the host and proxy, and the mail route's encryption. | Email channel: replies out, inbound by webhook |

## Outside the application

These stay with the laboratory and whoever hosts the deployment, whatever the application does:
the risk analysis and risk management (§164.308(a)(1)(ii)(A)-(B)), the sanction policy, security
awareness and training, the contingency plan with backups, disaster recovery and emergency mode
operation (§164.308(a)(7)), physical safeguards for facilities, workstations and media (§164.310),
policies and their documentation (§164.316), and the retention of required documentation for six
years (§164.316(b)(2), §164.530(j)). [Operations](operations.md) covers backups, restores, logs
and the deployment settings the application reads.
